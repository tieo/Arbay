Arbay identity · concept round

Nineteen marks, five arguments

Arbay watches a dozen marketplaces across borders and tells you where the same thing costs less. Five different arguments about what that deserves to look like: the name itself, the spread, many into one, the listing, and watching and reaching. Every mark is drawn from solid shapes with one accent, tested at launcher size, and shown as a one-color silhouette on both grounds — because a mark that only survives in full color is not a logo yet.

How to read the bands

App tile is the mark on its own colour, rounded like an adaptive icon. One-colour drops the palette: the accent becomes a knockout, stays solid, or hands its job to the form — a tint, a dotted fill, a shape held apart — so the one thing being pointed at survives without colour. That band is the honest one — it is what a monochrome status bar, an embroidered patch, and a favicon all get.

Construction rules held

No hairlines, no glyph floating unattached on a tile, one accent per mark, and every silhouette closed. Offset shadows are used only where two shapes need separating in depth, never as decoration.

Next step
